Targeting a 5G-green venue ✦

Task-sufficient retrieval
for greener 5G QA

A question answering system that decides, per question, exactly how much web evidence it needs, stops as soon as it has enough, and moves fewer bytes and spends less energy over a 5G link — without losing answer quality. Built entirely on local models, no hosted LLM API anywhere in the pipeline.

Built on local, open models
  • flan-t5-base + LoRA
  • MiniLM bi-encoder
  • RoBERTa QA
  • scikit-learn SGD
  • LinUCB
  • Linear Thompson sampling
The idea, in plain terms

Most retrieval-augmented systems either load a fixed amount of context or load everything they can and let the model sort it out. Both waste data. EcoBudget asks a more careful question: not "can it answer," but what is the smallest amount of loading that still answers correctly — and whether the energy saved by loading less actually beats the energy spent running the models.

  1. Decompose. Break a question into small, explicit information needs — a requirement, written as an (entity, attribute) pair, for example (iPhone 16, price).
  2. Retrieve. For each requirement, gate candidate passages to the right entity first, then rank by the attribute.
  3. Decide. A learned stopping policy decides after each retrieval whether it already has enough evidence (STOP) or should pull one more passage (RETRIEVE).
  4. Answer and measure. Generate the answer from only the evidence actually gathered, then measure the bytes moved and the energy that implies on a 5G access path.
System

What the pipeline is made of

A chain of small, independent, dependency-injected components, each unit tested without downloading a model.

Decomposer

decomposer.py — a fine-tuned flan-t5-base with a LoRA adapter that turns a question into requirements, snapped to a closed vocabulary of 35 attributes.

Retriever

retriever.py — a MiniLM bi-encoder over a pre-embedded corpus. Production retriever is the entity-aware two-stage version: gate to the entity, then rank by the attribute.

Evidence tracker

evidence.py — scores whether gathered passages satisfy each requirement using a RoBERTa QA confidence signal, since raw similarity is fooled by passages that mention the entity but not the attribute.

Stopping policy

bandit.py — a per-action contextual bandit (linear SGD classifier) trained with a per-step reward that trades marginal evidence coverage against byte cost.

Answer generator

answer.py — a fine-tuned flan-t5-base with a LoRA adapter, called once per requirement, with an abstain path.

Judge

judge.py — an answer-type-aware checker with a symmetric formatting normalizer, reporting judge_success (binary) and fact_f1 separately.

Energy model

energy.py — model FLOPs, 5G per-bit transfer energy, realistic payload scenarios, and an RRC radio-state and tail energy model.

System orchestrator

system.py — the end-to-end deployed path that wires every component above together.

Roadmap, phase by phase

The work is planned as phases A through H. Here is the honest status of each one, including the parts that didn't go as first expected.

A

Compute vs. transfer accounting done

At short-snippet payloads compute dominates transfer by roughly 100×, so saving bytes alone saves almost nothing at that scale — yet the policy still wins on total energy via fewer evidence-scoring inferences.

B

A defensible 5G energy model done

Replaced a generic web-CO2 number with a 5G per-bit model split into radio access, transport, and device modem receive. At realistic page scale, transfer dominates and loading fewer pages becomes the primary lever.

C

Dataset scale-up done

186 passages, 344 hand-authored seed tasks, 1376 total tasks, frozen train/val/test splits grouped by seed with a coverage invariant enforced in code.

D

Entity-aware retriever done

Fixed entity dominance with a two-stage retriever — gate to the entity, then rank by attribute. recall@1 rose from 0.914 to 0.989, recall@5 reached a perfect 1.000.

E

External baselines & frozen test run done

Added LinUCB, linear Thompson sampling, and an Adaptive-RAG analog. All adaptive methods converge to the same success at the same bytes — the SGD bandit is unstable across seeds, so LinUCB is recommended as the headline learned policy.

F

Radio-state & tail energy done

Added an RRC radio-state model — one promotion per query, radio held active across the retrieval loop, then a tail at tail power. Fewer fetches win on both energy and latency, with no tradeoff between the two.

G

Consolidation partly done

Re-ran the headline experiment on the entity-aware retriever, found and honestly fixed a byte-penalty regression, and re-selected lambda by a pre-registered sweep. A consolidated ablation table and reproducibility appendix remain.

H

Paper writeup not started

Related work, the Pareto and radio figures, and limitations.

Honesty, by design

What I report plainly, not just the wins

The point of this project is a defensible energy story, not a flattering one.

Does the learned policy beat a hand-tuned heuristic?▾

Not under the entity-aware retriever. With recall@1 at 0.989 there is little uncertainty left to exploit, so the bandit converges to the same policy as the heuristic (difference 0, confidence interval [0, 0]). It still clearly dominates fixed budgets and full-load, and its edge over the heuristic grows with retrieval uncertainty — that relationship is characterized directly in the noise sweep.

Is the custom SGD bandit a better algorithm than standard baselines?▾

No. On validation, the SGD bandit, LinUCB, LinTS, and the heuristic all reach the same success at the same bytes. Over five seeds the SGD bandit is unstable (0.790 ± 0.283, collapsing on some seeds), while LinUCB (0.913 ± 0.007) and LinTS (0.897 ± 0.014) are stable. LinUCB is recommended as the headline learned policy for that reason.

Does compute or transfer energy dominate?▾

It depends on payload realism. At short-snippet scale, compute dominates transfer by roughly 100×. At realistic web-page scale, transfer dominates and loading fewer pages becomes the primary energy lever. The policy wins in both regimes, for different reasons.

Does any part of this depend on a hosted LLM API?▾

No. Every model runs locally — flan-t5-base with LoRA, a MiniLM bi-encoder, and a RoBERTa QA confidence model. This is enforced in code: a script scans for banned imports and known API hosts, and a test runs it as part of the suite.

What's still open?▾

A consolidated ablation table and reproducibility appendix for Phase G, and the paper writeup itself in Phase H. Two known ceilings: narrative tasks have no training coverage and currently fail, and two retrieval near-synonyms still miss at rank 1.
