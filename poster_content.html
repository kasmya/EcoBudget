B1  -  New contribution: Energy-Aware Stopping Reward

The stopping controller previously optimized success minus a byte penalty. Bytes on the extracted snippets are ~102 B, so that penalty is nearly free - the policy barely feels retrieval cost. We reformulated the reward to charge each retrieval its true 5G energy: a fetch wakes the radio (RRC promotion + active + inactivity tail), a fixed ~107 J per page fetch under fast-dormancy. The policy now stops to save joules, not bytes.

B2  -  New contribution: Carbon-Intensity-Aware Scheduling

Joules become carbon via grid intensity - which is not constant: it swings ~2.3× over a day (deep midday solar trough, sharp evening peak). The same query costs very different carbon depending on when it runs. For latency-tolerant agent retrieval we shift each query to the greenest hour within a slack window. This is a time axis orthogonal to Idea 1, and the savings stack on top of it.

Novel Contributions (optional callout box for the poster)

Beyond task-sufficient retrieval, EcoBudget introduces two 5G-specific sustainability levers:

  • Carbon-aware scheduling - shifting latency-tolerant retrieval into greener grid hours to cut per-query CO2e a further 14-28% (4-12 h slack), stacking on top of stopping.
Place this as a small highlighted box near your Key Findings so reviewers see the new angle first. These are what distinguish this work from prior task-sufficient / adaptive-RAG retrieval.

Introduction

QA agents retrieve broadly and load far more of the web than a task needs, wasting data transfer, energy, and carbon. EcoBudget treats retrieval as task-level resource allocation: it decomposes a question into its information requirements, retrieves only for those, and stops once the evidence is sufficient - matching full-retrieval task success while transferring far fewer bytes and joules over 5G.

Research Gap & Questions (Objectives)

Research gap. Retrieval research optimizes relevance ranking - what to load - but not task-sufficiency, how much to load; and "green AI" work rarely models the 5G access path (RRC radio-state and tail energy) or the time-varying carbon intensity of the electricity grid.
Research question. Can an agent retrieve only the evidence a task requires, and stop when enough is enough, to cut data transfer, energy, and carbon over 5G without losing task success?
Hypothesis. Requirement-aware retrieval plus a learned stopping policy holds full-retrieval task success while transferring substantially fewer bytes and joules.
Objectives.
  • Decompose questions into (entity, attribute) requirements; retrieve per requirement.
  • Learn a STOP/RETRIEVE controller trading success against transfer cost.
  • Measure 5G energy and carbon (transfer + RRC radio-state model).
  • Optimize the controller for energy; schedule flexible retrieval into greener grid hours.

EcoBudget Approach

Retrieve only the evidence needed to complete the task, and stop when extra retrieval adds no value:
  1. Understand the task - identify the information requirements needed to answer it.
  2. Collect evidence - retrieve targeted to those requirements, not broadly.
  3. Measure sufficiency - track evidence coverage and QA-confidence against the data already transferred.
  4. Decide - STOP if the evidence is sufficient, RETRIEVE if more is worthwhile.
  5. Balance success and cost - maximize task success while minimizing unnecessary data transfer, energy, and carbon.

System Architecture & Methodology

Input: a user question and task type.
  1. Decompose into (entity, attribute) information requirements - fine-tuned flan-t5-base + LoRA, constrained-decoded (snapped) to a closed 35-attribute vocabulary.
  2. Retrieve per requirement - MiniLM bi-encoder, entity-aware two-stage: gate to the entity, then rank by attribute.
  3. Measure evidence sufficiency - RoBERTa QA-confidence per requirement, not raw similarity.
  4. Adaptive stopping controller - a contextual bandit (LinUCB) chooses STOP or RETRIEVE, trained on a measured-5G-energy reward; loop back to step 2 on RETRIEVE.
  5. Answer generation - from only the gathered evidence.
  6. Carbon-aware scheduler - defer latency-tolerant queries to low-carbon grid windows.
Output: an answer, plus the measured bytes, energy, and carbon it cost.

5G Relevance

5G enables high-throughput, data-intensive applications; at scale, their unnecessary application-layer transfer becomes an efficiency problem. EcoBudget acts on the first link in that chain - the bytes an application requests - which lowers the network traffic and the RRC radio-active time a task generates, yielding energy and carbon benefits. Beyond cutting bytes, EcoBudget adds a temporal lever for edge/5G deployments: scheduling flexible retrieval into low-carbon grid windows reduces per-query CO2e by 14-28% (4-12 h slack) with no loss of task success. Application areas: Sustainable Web, 5G / Edge Networks, AI Agents & Search.

Main Results

  • Retrieval: right fact on the first try 98.9% (vs 58.9% for whole-question); top-5 perfect.
  • Decomposition: correct (entity, attribute) split 89.2% (49.6% without snap-to-vocab).
  • When to stop: full-retrieval quality (0.895; yes/no 64/64) at ~75% fewer bytes.
  • Energy: ~66 J/query saved (95% CI 41-95); ~4× less 5G radio.
  • Live web: 92.8% and 97.8% fewer bytes.
  • (new) Greener stopping: same 0.895 at 4.1× lower radio energy.
  • (new) Greener timing: 14-28% less carbon in cleaner-grid hours.

Supporting Results

  • Snap-to-vocabulary ablation: constrained decoding onto the 35-attribute vocabulary raises decomposer exact-match from 0.496 to 0.892.
  • Policy stability: LinUCB is the deployed controller and is stable across seeds (0.913 ± 0.007); the custom SGD bandit is seed-unstable (0.790 ± 0.283) and is kept only as an ablation.
  • Compute vs transfer: compute dominates by about 100× at snippet scale, but transfer becomes about 95% of per-query energy at realistic page scale, where 5G radio energy under fast-dormancy is about 4× lower for adaptive stopping than full loading.
  • Reward-shaping ablation (new): a 5G-energy reward (per-fetch RRC radio-wake cost, about 107 J per page) matches the byte-reward policy's success (0.895) at equal-or-lower radio energy (25.9 vs 26.0 J) - showing the stopping policy is near energy-optimal, not merely byte-optimal.
  • Rigor: not overfit (train 0.850 vs test 0.867); zero split leakage; QA-confidence calibrated (ECE 0.077, Brier 0.096); measured median end-to-end latency 0.61 s (p95 0.86 s) over 128 frozen-test tasks.

Key Findings

  1. Requirement-aware retrieval wins: targeting each (entity, attribute) lifts first-try accuracy 0.914 → 0.989 (top-5 to 1.000).
  2. Decomposition is learnable: the fine-tuned model splits questions correctly 89.2%; snap-to-vocab adds ~0.40.
  3. Stopping early is nearly free: full-retrieval quality (0.895) at ~75% fewer bytes and ~66 J/query less, beating fixed budgets.
  4. (new) Energy is the stopping lever: a measured-joule reward holds 0.895 at 4.1× lower 5G radio energy than full retrieval.
  5. (new) Timing cuts carbon: grid intensity varies ~2.3×/day, so running flexible queries in greener hours saves a further 14-28%.

Discussion

EcoBudget shows that web retrieval can be treated as a task-level resource-allocation problem, not only a relevance-ranking problem. Requirement-aware retrieval improves the quality of retrieved evidence, because targeting a specific (entity, attribute) surfaces the right fact instead of the most entity-similar text. Adaptive stopping provides the mechanism to avoid retrieval that no longer changes the answer: most requirements are satisfied by one or two passages, so further retrieval adds data cost without adding coverage. With a strong retriever the learned controller matches a hand-tuned heuristic, and its advantage grows as retrieval uncertainty rises. Reframing the reward in measured joules shows the controller is already near the energy-optimal frontier, and carbon-aware scheduling adds an orthogonal temporal lever - together framing sustainability as both how much to load and when to load it.

Conclusion

EcoBudget treats retrieval as deciding how much to load, not just what is relevant. Requirement-aware retrieval improves evidence quality, and adaptive stopping halts once evidence is sufficient, trading task success against data-transfer cost. On a frozen test set it holds 0.895 success at about 75% fewer bytes than full-page loading. Optimizing the controller for measured energy and scheduling retrieval for grid carbon extend it from byte-efficiency to end-to-end energy- and carbon-efficiency. The contribution is a task-sufficiency framework for data-efficient retrieval, a step toward sustainable 5G access. Retrieve enough; stop when enough is enough.

Practical Implications

  • AI applications: retrieve according to what the task needs, not how much information is available.
  • Network efficiency: reducing unnecessary application-layer transfer lowers the data that retrieval-intensive apps request from the network.
  • Sustainable computing: selective retrieval saves about 66 J per query at the measured 506 KB page (95% CI 41-95) and about 4× 5G radio energy, and carbon-aware scheduling cuts a further 14-28% of per-query CO2e - measured directly, not deferred to future work.
